\section{System Model and Problem Definition}
\label{sec:system_model}

% We consider a fully decentralized vehicular learning system operating over discrete decision rounds $t=0,1,\dots,T-1$. Let $\mathcal{V}_t$ denote the set of active vehicles at round $t$. Due to mobility, the set of vehicles and their communication opportunities vary over time.

We consider a fully decentralized vehicular learning system in which connected vehicles collaboratively train personalized models over a hybrid 5G C-V2X network. Each vehicle aims to learn a model adapted to its own local context while selectively incorporating updates from statistically compatible vehicles. Collaboration is performed without any central coordination and relies on both PC5 sidelink and 5G Uu communication links, which exhibit different range, latency, and cost characteristics. In the following, we formalize the network topology enabling personalized training and its associated computation and communication cost model.

\subsection{Collaboration Graph}

We consider a time-varying vehicular network observed over discrete decision rounds $t \in \{0,\dots,T-1\}$. At each round $t$, let $\mathcal{V}_t$ denote the set of active vehicles participating in collaborative learning. Since vehicle locations and wireless connectivity evolve continuously, the communication topology is inherently dynamic. Model exchange is supported by a hybrid V2X network with two communication modes: direct PC5 sidelink and infrastructure-assisted 5G Uu relay. We represent feasible communication opportunities by the graph
%
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{G}_t^{\mathrm{feas}} = \bigl(\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{sl}},\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{uu}}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
%
where $(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{sl}}$ if a direct PC5 transmission between vehicles $i$ and $j$ is feasible at round $t$, and $(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{uu}}$ if a 5G Uu path through the infrastructure is available. For each pair $(i,j)$, we define the feasible communication mode set as
%
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{M}_{ij}^{(t)} = \{m\in\{\mathrm{sl},\mathrm{uu}\}:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_t^{m}\}.
\end{equation*}
%
The feasible neighbor set of vehicle $i$ is then
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{F}_i^{(t)} = \{j\in\mathcal{V}_t\setminus\{i\}:\mathcal{M}_{ij}^{(t)}\neq\emptyset\}.
\end{equation*}
%
The actual collaboration is modeled by the directed graph
%
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{G}_t^{\mathrm{coll}} = \bigl(\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{coll}}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
%
where $(j\!\to\! i)\in\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{coll}}$ indicates that vehicle $i$ uses the update received from vehicle $j$ at round $t$. The collaboration neighborhood of vehicle $i$ is therefore
%
\begin{equation*}
    C_i^{(t)} = \{j\in\mathcal{F}_i^{(t)}:(j\!\to\! i)\in\mathcal{E}_t^{\mathrm{coll}}\}.
\end{equation*}
%
For each active collaboration edge, vehicle $i$ selects a communication mode $m_{ij}^{(t)}\in\mathcal{M}_{ij}^{(t)}$ and assigns a collaboration weight $\alpha_{ij}^{(t)}\geq 0$, subject to
%
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{j\in C_i^{(t)}} \alpha_{ij}^{(t)} \leq 1,
    \qquad
    \alpha_{ii}^{(t)} = 1 - \sum_{j\in C_i^{(t)}} \alpha_{ij}^{(t)}.
\end{equation*}
%
Hence, vehicle $i$ always retains a fraction of its own model while selectively incorporating updates from its collaborators. The collaboration graph is asymmetric and overlapping, that is, $j\in C_i^{(t)}$ does not imply $i\in C_j^{(t)}$.

\subsection{Learning Model}
\label{subsec:learning}

Each vehicle $i\in\mathcal{V}_t$ holds a private dataset $\mathcal{D}_i=\{(x_{i,k},y_{i,k})\}_{k=1}^{n_i}$ drawn from a local distribution $P_i$. The distributions $\{P_i\}$ are heterogeneous across vehicles, yielding non-IID data. Vehicle $i$ maintains a personalized model $w_i^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and aims to minimize its population risk
%
\begin{equation*}
    F_i(w)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P_i}\bigl[\ell(w;x,y)\bigr].
\end{equation*}
%
In practice, since $P_i$ is unknown, local optimization is performed on the empirical risk
%
\begin{equation*}
    \hat{F}_i(w)=\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{k=1}^{n_i}\ell(w;x_{i,k},y_{i,k}).
\end{equation*}
%
The personalized objective of vehicle $i$ is therefore
\begin{equation*}
    \min_{w_i} F_i(w_i).
\end{equation*}
%
Since $P_i\neq P_j$ in general, enforcing $w_i=w_j$ for all vehicles is suboptimal. At the system level, the objective is to learn the set of personalized models $W=\{w_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{V}_t}$ by minimizing the aggregate multi-task loss
\begin{equation*}
    \min_W \mathcal{L}(W) = \sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_t}\rho_i F_i(w_i),
    \qquad
    \rho_i\geq 0,
    \quad
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_t}\rho_i=1,
\end{equation*}
%
where $\rho_i$ denotes the weight of vehicle $i$. Unlike classical Decentralized Learning (DL), this objective does not impose model consensus.

% At the beginning of round $t$, vehicle $i$ aggregates its model with the models received from its collaborators:
At the beginning of round $t$, vehicle $i$ receives the most recent models $\hat{w}_j^{(t)}$ from its collaborators $j\in C_i^{(t)}$ and forms the aggregate model $\bar{w}_i^{(t)}$.
%
\begin{equation}
    \bar{w}_i^{(t)} = \alpha_{ii}^{(t)} w_i^{(t)} + \sum_{j\in C_i^{(t)}} \alpha_{ij}^{(t)} \hat{w}_j^{(t)},
\end{equation}
%
where $\hat{w}_j^{(t)}$ is the model received from vehicle $j$, and
%
\begin{equation*}
    \alpha_{ii}^{(t)}+\sum_{j\in C_i^{(t)}}\alpha_{ij}^{(t)}=1,
    \qquad
    \alpha_{ij}^{(t)}\geq 0.
\end{equation*}
%
Starting from $\bar{w}_i^{(t)}$, vehicle $i$ performs $I_i^{(t)}$ local SGD steps on mini-batches of size $b_i$ sampled from $\mathcal{D}_i$:
%
\begin{equation}
    w_i^{(t,\ell+1)} = w_i^{(t,\ell)} - \eta_i^{(t)} g_i\!\left(w_i^{(t,\ell)};\xi_i^{(t,\ell)}\right),
\end{equation}
%
for $\ell=0,\dots,I_i^{(t)}-1$, with $w_i^{(t,0)}=\bar{w}_i^{(t)}$, where $\xi_i^{(t,\ell)}$ is a mini-batch of size $b_i$ drawn from $\mathcal{D}_i$ and $g_i(\cdot;\xi_i^{(t,\ell)})$ is the corresponding stochastic gradient of $\hat{F}_i$. The updated personalized model is then
%
\begin{equation*}
    w_i^{(t+1)} = w_i^{(t,I_i^{(t)})}.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Computation and Communication Model}

Each vehicle incurs both local computation cost and V2X communication cost during collaborative training. We model these costs at the granularity of
one learning round.

If vehicle $i$ performs $I_i^{(t)}$ local SGD steps on mini-batches of size $b_i$, with $c_i$ CPU cycles per sample and processor frequency
$f_i^{(t)}$, its local computation time and energy are
%
\begin{equation*}
    \tau_{i,\mathrm{cmp}}^{(t)} = \frac{I_i^{(t)}\, b_i\, c_i}{f_i^{(t)}},
    \qquad
    E_{i,\mathrm{cmp}}^{(t)} = \kappa I_i^{(t)}\, b_i\, c_i \bigl(f_i^{(t)}\bigr)^2
\end{equation*}
%
where $\kappa$ is the effective switched capacitance.

Vehicles exchange model updates over hybrid 5G C-V2X links, either through direct PC5 sidelink or infrastructure-assisted 5G Uu relay, which exhibit
different range, latency, and power characteristics \cite{garcia2021tutorial,molina2017ltev,3gpp2021architecture}. Let $S$ denote the model payload size in bits. For a PC5 sidelink transmission from vehicle $j$ to vehicle $i$, the achievable rate is
%
\begin{equation*}
    R_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)} = B_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)} \log_2\!\bigl(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
%
with transmission time and energy
%
\begin{equation*}
    \tau_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)}=\frac{S}{R_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)}},
    \qquad
    E_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)}=p_{j,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)}\tau_{ji,\mathrm{sl}}^{(t)}.
\end{equation*}
%
For a 5G Uu relay path through the serving gNB, the end-to-end transfer time is determined by the uplink and downlink legs:
\begin{equation*}
    \tau_{ji,\mathrm{uu}}^{(t)} = \frac{S}{R_{j\rightarrow g}^{u,(t)}} + \frac{S}{R_{g\rightarrow i}^{d,(t)}},
\end{equation*}
%
and the associated communication energy is
%
\begin{equation*}
    E_{ji,\mathrm{uu}}^{(t)} = p_j^{u,(t)}\frac{S}{R_{j\rightarrow g}^{u,(t)}} + p_i^{\mathrm{rx},(t)}\frac{S}{R_{g\rightarrow i}^{d,(t)}}.
\end{equation*}
%
This model captures the core asymmetry of hybrid V2X collaboration: PC5 is typically cheaper and lower-latency but range-limited, whereas 5G Uu relay
offers larger reach at higher communication cost.

\subsection{Threat Model}

This work considers an open vehicular environment in which a subset $\mathcal{B}_t\subseteq\mathcal{V}_t$ may behave in a Byzantine manner at round $t$. A Byzantine vehicle is assumed to know the protocol, collude with other Byzantine vehicles, and send arbitrary, inconsistent, or strategically poisoned model updates, but it cannot directly compromise the local computation of honest vehicles. The threat model covers crash and communication failures, free-riding or lazy participants that consume updates without contributing useful models, and adversarial model poisoning aimed at introducing backdoors, causing divergence, or steering learning toward ineffective solutions. We particularly consider the case where Byzantine vehicles collude to dominate the neighborhood of an honest vehicle and corrupt its personalized model. To mitigate the effect of unreliable or Byzantine neighbors, each vehicle maintains a local trust score $q_{ij}^{(t)}\in[0,1]$ for every feasible neighbor $j\in\mathcal{F}_i^{(t)}$. This score represents vehicle $i$'s current estimate of the reliability of $j$'s transmitted model updates.
