\begin{abstract}
	Connected vehicles increasingly rely on collaborative intelligence for services such as cooperative perception, risk prediction, and personalized driver assistance. Realizing such intelligence requires learning from distributed, privacy-sensitive, and strongly non-IID data under stringent latency, energy, and bandwidth constraints. Existing federated and edge-assisted methods remain poorly suited to this setting: they rely on centralized orchestration, assume a single communication interface, and offer limited resilience to unreliable peers. We study robust decentralized personalized learning over hybrid 5G C-V2X networks, whose PC5 sidelink and Uu interfaces offer complementary range, latency, and cost profiles for resource-aware collaboration. We formulate collaboration as a dynamic directed network formation game in which each vehicle chooses its collaborators, communication mode, and aggregation weights under local resource and trust constraints. Based on this formulation, we propose \textsc{Dante}, a fully decentralized framework in which every vehicle learns a link-formation policy through an attention-guided actor--critic, aggregates received models under fused attention--trust--robustness weights, and maintains online local trust estimates for its peers. We analyze convergence and per-round complexity, and SUMO-driven experiments over real urban and highway maps show that \textsc{Dante} delivers substantial resource savings and more efficient collaboration while preserving personalized accuracy and Byzantine robustness over representative baselines.
\end{abstract}
